## Software process assessment

Software process assessment is about evaluating the way a team develops software: are our
current practices actually working? It examines existing practices and whether those practices
are producing good results.

This is important because a team may follow a process and still produce software that users are
not happy with. Assessment is what reveals that gap, so the way software is produced can be
improved.

Assessment compares actual practice against standard, expected or recommended ways of doing
software development.

**Purpose, threefold:**
1. Evaluate whether current practices are producing good results.
2. Determine process maturity. A mature process is stable, predictable and well-controlled.
3. Identify where improvement is needed against standards.

## Software Process Improvement

SPI is making the software development process stronger and more effective.

**Three key activities:**
1. **Identifying process weaknesses.** Studying the current process to find what is not working:
   delays, frequent errors, poor communication, inefficient procedures.
2. **Introducing improvements.** Changing procedures, adopting better tools, improving team
   practices, restructuring workflows.
3. **Monitoring the impact.** Checking whether the changes actually improved the process. If
   not, adjusting again.

SPI rests on continuous improvement: processes are never considered perfect or finished; they
are always being reviewed, refined and improved over time.

## Process measurement

Process measurement means collecting and analysing data about how software development processes
are performing. Instead of saying "the process is slow" or "testing is difficult", real data is
used: "testing now takes 5 days instead of the expected 2", "we are recording about 50 bugs per
week".

Without measurement, any attempt to improve a software process becomes guesswork rather than a
structured engineering activity.

**Purpose:** evaluate process performance; identify inefficiencies where time, effort or
resources are wasted; track improvements over time; support decision-making with real data.

**Common process metrics:** defect rate; time taken to complete tasks; cost of development;
rework effort (how much work must be repeated due to errors or changes).

**Importance:** objectivity, meaning decisions based on facts and numbers rather than personal
opinion; evidence-based decisions; the ability to track progress over time.

## CMMI

CMMI (Capability Maturity Model Integration) is a structured framework used to evaluate how good
an organisation's software development process is and how it can be improved. It answers: how
mature is our current process, and how do we make it better.

Its main purpose is to help organisations improve processes, enhance software quality and
increase development efficiency. It is not only about assessment but also about continuous
improvement.

**Process maturity** refers to how developed and well-organised a software development process
is. A mature organisation follows clearly defined processes, produces consistent results and
continuously improves its way of working.

### The five levels

**Level 1: Initial.** The process is chaotic and unstructured. There are no standard procedures
and everything depends on individual effort. Results are unpredictable and quality is usually
poor.

**Level 2: Managed.** The organisation begins to introduce order. Work is planned and tracked
and basic project management practices are used. Requirements are managed and progress is
monitored, bringing some level of control.

**Level 3: Defined.** Processes are clearly documented and standardised across the entire
organisation. Everyone follows the same procedures and training is provided to ensure
consistency. This leads to more predictable and reliable outcomes.

**Level 4: Quantitatively Managed.** The organisation uses data and metrics to control its
processes. Instead of relying on assumptions, decisions are based on actual numbers. This makes
performance more predictable and improves control.

**Level 5: Optimizing.** The highest level of maturity. The focus is continuous improvement.
Processes are constantly refined based on feedback and performance data. Innovation is
encouraged and problems are prevented before they occur.

**Importance:** provides a roadmap for improvement; enhances product quality; increases customer
confidence; improves the predictability of software projects.

## ISO/IEC 12207

ISO/IEC 12207 is a globally accepted standard that defines what processes should exist when
developing software from start to finish. It provides a clear framework so that organisations do
not each do things in their own random way.

**Purpose, threefold:** establish a common structure for software processes; clearly define the
activities involved in software development and maintenance; ensure consistency across different
organisations and projects, improving coordination and quality.

**Three categories of process:**

**Primary processes.** The core activities directly involved in building software: requirements
analysis (understanding what the system should do), design (planning the system structure),
implementation (writing the code), testing (checking for errors), and maintenance (fixing and
updating after deployment).

**Supporting processes.** Not part of direct development, but they support it: documentation,
configuration management (controlling changes in the system), quality assurance, and
verification and validation.

**Organizational processes.** Focused on management and long-term improvement: process
improvement activities, training of staff, and infrastructure management ensuring tools and
environments are available and properly maintained.

**Importance:** standardises software processes so organisations follow a structured approach;
improves communication among teams because everyone understands the same framework; ensures
quality and consistency across projects.

## Process optimization

Process optimization means improving and refining the software development process so it becomes
faster, more efficient and more effective.

**Goals:** reduce development time without sacrificing quality; minimise costs so fewer
resources are wasted; improve product quality; increase productivity so the team produces more
value in less time.

**Techniques:**

1. **Automation of tasks.** Using tools and software to perform repetitive tasks automatically.
   Automated testing tools run tests without human intervention; CI/CD tools automatically build
   and deploy software. This reduces human error, saves time and speeds up development.
2. **Adoption of best practices.** Using proven methods known to produce good results: writing
   clean and structured code, following coding standards, using methodologies such as Agile.
   These improve quality, reduce mistakes and ensure consistency.
3. **Continuous monitoring and feedback.** Constantly observing how the process is performing and
   gathering feedback from users and team members, so problems are detected early and corrected
   before they become bigger issues.
4. **Use of metrics for decision-making.** Using real data and measurements to guide decisions
   instead of guessing: bug frequency, development time, system performance.

## The relationship between assessment and improvement

Process assessment tells us what is wrong; process improvement tells us how to fix it.

During assessment, the process is examined and weaknesses identified: delays, inefficiencies,
poor quality practices, lack of standards. During improvement, those weaknesses are addressed by
introducing better methods, tools or practices.

Assessment feeds improvement, and improvement depends on assessment. The relationship forms a
continuous cycle:

**Measure -> Analyze -> Improve -> Repeat**

Measure the process using data and metrics. Analyse the data to understand what is working and
what is not. Improve the process by making necessary changes. Repeat the cycle again and again.
It is not a one-time activity; it is continuous.
