# CMMI: CAPABILITY MATURITY MODEL INTEGRATION

A structured framework for evaluating how good an organisation's software development process is,
and how it can be improved. It answers two questions: how mature is our current process, and how
do we make it better.

**Process maturity** is how developed and well-organised a development process is. A mature
organisation follows clearly defined processes, produces consistent results, and continuously
improves.

---

## The five levels

### Level 1: Initial
The process is chaotic and unstructured. There are no standard procedures. Everything depends on
individual effort. Results are unpredictable and quality is usually poor.

*The tell:* project success depends on the working hours and heroics of individual senior
engineers. Workflows are unmapped and chaotic.

### Level 2: Managed
The organisation begins to introduce order. Work is planned and tracked. Basic project management
practices are used. Requirements are managed and progress is monitored, bringing some control.

*The tell:* basic project management constraints such as scheduling and project tracking are
functional, but processes are not uniform or standardised across the company.

### Level 3: Defined
Processes are clearly documented and standardised across the entire organisation. Everyone follows
the same procedures. Training is provided to ensure consistency. Outcomes become more predictable
and reliable.

*The tell:* every development team follows the same established processes, but the firm does not
use statistical models or quantitative data to control process performance.

### Level 4: Quantitatively Managed
The organisation uses data and metrics to control its processes. Decisions are based on actual
numbers rather than assumptions. Performance becomes more predictable and control improves.

*The tell:* quantitative data and statistical techniques are used to monitor and control process
performance.

### Level 5: Optimizing
The highest level. The focus is continuous improvement. Processes are constantly refined based on
feedback and performance data. Innovation is encouraged. Problems are prevented before they occur.

*The tell:* processes are continuously refined, enhanced and optimised based on performance data
feedback loops and innovative methods.

---

## The three boundaries

Most self-assessments err at one of these three.

### 1 to 2
Level 1 is heroics. Level 2 is planning and tracking. If the project would fail without one
specific person working late, it is level 1 regardless of the documentation.

### 2 to 3
Level 2 means each **project** is managed. Level 3 means the **organisation** has one standard
process that projects tailor from, with training to support it.

Per-project documented practice is still level 2. This is the most common over-claim.

### 3 to 4
The fundamental prerequisite for moving from Defined to Quantitatively Managed is that the
organisation **must begin managing, measuring and controlling process performance using
quantitative data and statistical techniques.**

Excellent documentation, standardised across the organisation, with no quantitative control, is
level 3. Having dashboards is not sufficient; the question is whether decisions are made from a
statistical understanding of process variation.

---

## Importance

Provides a roadmap for improvement. Enhances product quality. Increases customer confidence.
Improves the predictability of software projects.
